An invited seminar in the Ira A. Fulton Schools of Engineering IE Seminar Series,
given during my visiting scholarship at Arizona State University.

Most satellite operations still rely on centralized planning, which becomes a
bottleneck as constellations grow. The talk set out how Distributed Satellite
Systems let satellites collaborate directly &mdash; opening the door to more
resilient and efficient operations &mdash; and presented a decentralized task
allocation method formulated as a distributed optimization problem, in which
satellites evaluate observation capability through a reward function accounting
for energy availability, data storage, schedule conflicts and angular separation
from the target.

The approach minimises communication overhead through selective propagation, and
I discussed how different network structures, the presence of central nodes, and
the degree of centralisation influence responsiveness and coordination efficiency
at constellation sizes up to 10,000 satellites.
